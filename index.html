Questions frequently asked

Am I really a "first downstream operator"?

If a supplier passes a DDS reference number (or a simplified-declaration identifier) to you, the European Commission FAQ (5th iteration, no. 3.4) says you can assume you're the first downstream operator or trader — the obligation to pass numbers lies on the upstream operator, not on you to ask. You collect and keep the numbers; you don't file statements of your own.

When does this start?

Main obligations apply from 30 December 2026 for operators and downstream operators/traders of all sizes, with 30 June 2027 for natural persons and micro/small enterprises established by 31 December 2024 (Reg. (EU) 2025/2650) — except EUTR wood products, already covered from 30 December 2026. Records must be kept 5 years from the placing on the market.

Do we need geolocation or a geospatial platform?

No. That's the operator-side obligation (the importer's). Your duty as first downstream operator is collecting reference numbers, keeping supplier and customer master data, and retaining it five years. A ledger is the appropriate tool — this is the gap between the geospatial enterprise platforms (built for operators) and the €20k consulting engagements (built for nobody here).

What if a supplier sends us no number at all?

You may assume in good faith that the supplier is not an upstream operator (FAQ no. 3.4) — you don't have to investigate. If you later obtain information that a product may not comply, you must notify the competent authority immediately (Art. 5(5)) and, if you're a non-SME, verify before placing further products. The ledger keeps that trail.

Is this legal advice?

No. FirstDownstream keeps the operational record the regulation demands; it is not a law firm opinion. Your company remains responsible for the underlying facts — the reference numbers and master data you record.
